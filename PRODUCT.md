# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Find Work serves a small Chinese-speaking community looking for foreign-company China roles, APAC roles, and overseas remote jobs that are plausibly usable from China. Readers may come from different backgrounds, so they need fast scanning, clear eligibility signals, and low-friction filtering before they decide whether a role is worth opening.

The maintainer also uses the site to publish daily Markdown job picks, collect reader preferences through a survey, and review aggregate demand in a private admin view.

## Product Purpose

Find Work turns curated job-pick Markdown into a quiet, trustworthy static website with daily issues, archives, job filtering, a preference survey, and survey statistics. Success means readers can quickly understand whether a job fits their role, language level, experience stage, work mode, and China applicability without feeling like they are browsing a generic job board.

## Brand Personality

Practical, selective, human. The interface should feel like a careful person sharing useful leads with a known group, not like a recruitment marketplace or growth landing page.

## Anti-references

This should not look or feel like a traditional job board, an enterprise recruiting site, an AI-generated landing page, a crowded directory, or a WeChat article archive. Avoid generic SaaS hero grammar, dense link-farm layouts, corporate HR polish, inflated marketing claims, and visual treatments that compete with the job information.

## Design Principles

- Make fit visible early: surface role direction, work mode, language level, experience stage, application barrier, and China applicability before asking readers to open a listing.
- Protect trust through restraint: keep the interface calm, readable, and specific; do not exaggerate certainty when a job requires applicant-side confirmation.
- Preserve the small-community feeling: write and arrange content like a curated note for known readers, not like a public marketplace chasing volume.
- Favor scanning over decoration: hierarchy, filtering, and status feedback matter more than brand spectacle.
- Keep publishing lightweight: design decisions should respect the Markdown-to-static-site workflow and avoid maintenance-heavy content structures.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-operable navigation, filters, forms, and admin controls. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Labels, status messages, and form errors should be explicit in Chinese and understandable without relying on color alone.
